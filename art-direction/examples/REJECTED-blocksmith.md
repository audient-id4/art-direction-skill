# Rejected — Blocksmith as a blockout sheet

A real failure, kept because nothing about it looks like a mistake from the
inside. It followed every rule in this skill, scored well on the original
audit, and was about the wrong thing.

**Product.** Blocksmith, a platform for people who build game worlds — voxel
and block construction, mechanics, publishing worlds other people can play.

**Metaphor chosen.** The blockout sheet: the orthographic drawing a level
designer makes before geometry exists. Plan views, section cuts, dimension
lines, a margin of annotation.

**What it produced.** Drafting mylar with a 6 mm grid. Rotring line weights
as semantics. `SHT 01 — MASTHEAD`. `SECTION A—A`. Plates numbered `P-01 · REV
C`. Scale stamped `1:2000`. Apollo flight-plan monospace in the margins. An
Ordnance Survey legend in the footer.

Internally consistent. Genuinely well drawn. Wrong.

## Why it passed every check

This is the part worth studying.

The inspiration map was full and none of it came from a website — drafting
mylar, Mies poché, Kahn sections, Crouwel, a plotter pen drawing a line.
Twelve references, each with a stated transfer. Three directions were
compared and two rejected with reasons. The tokens all carried roles: four
radii, a base unit from the grid pitch, one accent. Contrast measured.
Reduced-motion handled. Contamination check clean — it resembles Linear,
Stripe and shadcn not at all.

Every countable test passed. The design was still about architectural
documentation.

## What went wrong

**The artifact came from the apparatus pool.** Drafting is how a level
designer works. It is not what anyone loves about building worlds. Asked for
a physical object from the domain, the professional instrument surfaced —
concrete, photogenic, easy to name — and the reflex went unexamined.

**The substitution test would have caught it in one minute.** Swap "worlds"
for "buildings" and the entire design still fits, unchanged. That is the
tell: the apparatus of drafting is shared across every industry that draws
things, which is exactly why the result felt so clean and so transferable.

**The emotional purchase was never written.** Had it been — *"turn a place
that exists in your head into somewhere other people can walk around"* —
nothing in the mylar direction expresses it. Mylar expresses *tolerance,
revision control and measurement*. All real virtues of the product. None of
them the reason anyone opens it.

**The name was ignored.** The product is called **Block**smith. The domain is
voxels: cubes, hand placement, a flat plain that becomes a structure because
someone stacked things on it for three hours. The obvious pool was never
entered, because "obvious" had been trained into a warning sign — and the
rule against obvious choices is about *layouts*, not about the subject matter
of the product itself.

**It substituted the maker's craft for the user's emotion.** The drafting
concept is a pleasure to design and a pleasure to read *as design*. That is
its own warning: a page whose main achievement is legible to designers has an
audience of designers.

## The correct read

The emotional purchase is making an impossible place real. The outcome pool
holds the artifact: the block itself, the moment a flat plain stops being
flat, a structure seen from inside for the first time.

Apparatus can still appear — a builder does want scale, snapping and
precision — but as *detail*, subordinate to the thing being built. Precision
in service of the world, not the world as evidence of precision.

## The lesson, in one line

An artifact from the apparatus pool always produces a coherent design, and
coherence is not the test. Ask what the person is buying emotionally, and
then ask whether the metaphor could survive being about someone else's
industry. If it could, it is.
